## What this section measures

An *arudha* is the reflected image of a house. *BPHS* Chapter 29 sets out the calculation:

> "The Pada of the ascendant will correspond to the sign arrived at by counting so many signs from the ascendant lord as he is away from the ascendant. Similarly Padas for other houses be known through their lords. The word 'Pada' exclusively denotes the Pada for ascendant (or Lagna Pada)."
> — BPHS 29.1–3

In Parasara's framing, the *arudha* of a house is found by counting the distance from the house to its lord, then counting that same distance again from the lord. The result is a sign that "mirrors" the house — the house's reflection at twice the distance from the lord.

Parasara's note in this chapter records that the *arudha* system originates in his own framework but was developed extensively by Jaimini:

> "Maharshi Jaimini (author of Poorva Mimamsa) extensively dealt with this system of astrology as given in chs. 29–33 hereof. Though sage Parasara has originally sown seeds, later on Jaimini delved deep and located many pearls out of his great wisdom and intuition."
> — BPHS 29 commentary

DashaflowView reports all twelve arudhas (A1 through A12) — the reflected images of each of the twelve houses.

## What each arudha represents

Each *arudha* takes its meaning from the house it reflects:

| Arudha | House reflected | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| A1 — Lagna Pada | First (self) | How the self is perceived; the public image of the body and identity |
| A2 — Dhana Pada | Second (wealth) | How wealth is perceived; reputation for resources |
| A3 — Vikrama Pada | Third (siblings, courage) | Perception of courage and siblings |
| A4 — Sukha Pada | Fourth (home, mother) | Perception of one's home life and emotional grounding |
| A5 — Mantra Pada | Fifth (children, intellect) | Perception of intellect, mantras, children |
| A6 — Roga Pada | Sixth (enemies, illness) | Perception of struggles, debt, illness |
| A7 — Dara Pada | Seventh (spouse) | Perception of one's marriage and partnerships |
| A8 — Mrityu Pada | Eighth (longevity, hidden) | Perception of mortality and the hidden |
| A9 — Bhagya Pada | Ninth (fortune, dharma) | Perception of one's fortune and dharma |
| A10 — Karma Pada | Tenth (career) | Perception of one's profession and standing |
| A11 — Labha Pada | Eleventh (gains) | Perception of gains and friends |
| A12 — Upa Pada | Twelfth (loss, liberation) | The Upapada — perception of loss and the marriage themes (treated separately in Chapter 30) |

## How the arudhas are read

The arudha reading turns on a fundamental distinction between the house and its arudha. The house describes the *thing*; the arudha describes the *appearance of the thing*. *BPHS* 29 and the Jaimini Sutras both treat the arudhas as the public, social, perceived dimension of a life area — what others see and what the chart-holder projects, rather than what the area actually is.

A reading typical in the tradition: a strong house with a weak arudha describes a life area that is genuinely strong but appears weak to others; a weak house with a strong arudha describes the reverse — actual struggle masked by a strong appearance. The two readings together describe both the substance and the surface of each life area.

Mutual relationships between arudhas — particularly Lagna Pada and Dara Pada (seventh-arudha) — are treated in the tradition as indicators of relationship dynamics. *BPHS* 29 toward the end of the chapter discusses these mutual positions:

> "If the Lagna Pada and Dara Pada are mutually angular or 3rd and 11th or trinal, the native will be a king ruling the earth."
> — BPHS 29 (closing slokas)
