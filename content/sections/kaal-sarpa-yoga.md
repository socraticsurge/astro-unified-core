## What this section measures

*Kaal Sarpa Yoga* is a modern formulation that flags charts where all seven non-nodal planets — Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn — fall on one side of the Rahu-Ketu axis. The "snake" imagery comes from the visual pattern: Rahu is the head, Ketu the tail, and all other planets are caught within the body.

The yoga is **not present in the canonical classical texts.** *BPHS*, *Saravali*, *Phaladeepika*, *Brihat Jataka*, the *Jaimini Sutras*, and *Sripati Paddhati* — the six texts in this library — none describe *Kaal Sarpa Yoga* under that name or any equivalent classical name. The formulation is a 20th-century construct, popularised in mainstream Indian astrology over the past several decades, with no corresponding sloka in the canonical literature.

The section renders only when the configuration is detected in the chart.

## What the section shows

When detected, the section reports the configuration and its standard modern reading. The *Kaal Sarpa* configurations are sometimes named in modern works — *Anant*, *Kulik*, *Vasuki*, *Shankhpal*, *Padma*, *Mahapadma*, *Takshak*, *Karkotak*, *Shankhachud*, *Ghatak*, *Vishdhar*, *Sheshnag* — based on which house Rahu occupies. The naming is a modern systematisation; the underlying classical authority is the general doctrine of nodal influence rather than the specific named yogas.

## How the section is read

A note on epistemology before the reading. Because *Kaal Sarpa Yoga* sits outside the classical canon, the tradition's working stance is divided. Some modern practitioners treat it as a significant chart feature requiring remediation; others treat it as a re-described version of nodal influence already covered by the standard rules for Rahu and Ketu in their respective placements. The *researcher's* position is to acknowledge the formulation, note its absence from the canonical texts, and let the reader weigh how much interpretive weight to put on a doctrine that does not appear in *BPHS* or *Saravali*.

The classical authority that *does* govern this configuration is the standard treatment of Rahu and Ketu under their houses, signs, and aspects. A chart with all planets between Rahu and Ketu is, in the classical reading, a chart where Rahu's house and Ketu's house carry concentrated weight — a concentration that already appears in the per-house and per-sign readings of the two nodes. The *Kaal Sarpa* framing names this concentration as a discrete yoga; the classical framing reads it as the natural consequence of Rahu and Ketu's existing significations.

## What is reported

DashaflowView reports the raw configuration when detected: which planets fall in which house range, which named *Kaal Sarpa* variant the configuration corresponds to (if applicable), and the exact angular positions of Rahu and Ketu relative to the other planets. The output is presented as data rather than as an interpreted reading, given the canonical-authority caveat above.

For a chart-holder whose chart triggers the *Kaal Sarpa* detection, the practical reading is to attend closely to the placements, dignities, and aspects of Rahu and Ketu — the underlying classical material — and to treat the *Kaal Sarpa* framing itself as a contemporary interpretive lens rather than a settled classical doctrine.
