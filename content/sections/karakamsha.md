## What this section measures

The Karakamsha is the sign occupied by the Atmakaraka — the soul-significator — in the Navamsa (D9) chart. It is read in the tradition as the orientation of the soul in this birth: the spiritual direction the chart-holder is built to pursue.

The reading combines two strands of classical authority. *BPHS* Chapter 33 (Effects of Karakamsa) covers the placement extensively from the Parashari side. The Jaimini Sutras treat the same calculation under their own tradition, with substantial overlap and some interpretive divergence. DashaflowView's calculation follows the Parashari method while reporting the Jaimini-tradition Atmakaraka as the input.

## How the Karakamsha is computed

The Atmakaraka is the planet at the highest degree in its sign among the seven non-nodal planets, computed in the Jaimini Karakas section above. The Atmakaraka's placement in the Navamsa chart gives the Karakamsha sign.

For example, if Saturn is the Atmakaraka and falls in Aquarius in the Navamsa, the Karakamsha is Aquarius. The house in the Navamsa (or, more commonly, the house in the rasi chart counted from the lagna) where this sign falls is the Karakamsha house.

## How the section is read

Classical authority treats Karakamsha as the most personally telling placement for spiritual orientation. The sign of Karakamsha tells the kind of practice the soul is built for; the planets in Karakamsha indicate forces that shape that practice; aspects to Karakamsha qualify it.

The Karakamsha reading is composite with the rasi chart: a strong Karakamsha placement in the Navamsa supports the dharmic readings of the D9 chart and the readings of the lagna's ninth house in the rasi. A weak or afflicted Karakamsha indicates struggle with the soul-direction it points to, not absence of the orientation.

The Ishta Devata reading is treated practically — the lord of the 12th from Karakamsha names the form of practice or deity the chart-holder is most aligned with. Different schools of practice within the Vedic tradition emphasise different aspects of this derivation; the calculation here follows the standard Parashari–Jaimini convention.
