## What this section measures

The lagna is the sign rising in the east at the moment of birth. It fixes the first house and the orientation of every other house in the chart. Among all chart values, the lagna is the most birth-time-sensitive: about one degree shifts every four minutes, so a small error in the recorded time can move the lagna into the next sign and reorganise the whole chart.

The fourteen divisional lagnas (D2 Hora through D60 Shastiamsa) are the same ascendant computed in fractional zodiacs that classical authority assigns to specific life themes. Each varga subdivides the rasi chart into a finer-grained zodiac, and the lagna in that varga is read alongside the lagna in the rasi chart. The divisional lagnas sharpen the reading of the corresponding house in the rasi chart rather than replacing it.

## The sixteen vargas

Parasara opens *BPHS* Chapter 6 by enumerating the sixteen divisions:

> "Lord Brahma has described 16 kinds of Vargas (Divisions) for each sign. Listen to those. The same are: Rasi, Hora, Drekkana, Chathurthamsa, Sapthamamsa, Navamsa, Dasamamsa, Dvadasamsa, Shodasamsa, Vimsamsa, Chaturvimsamsa, Sapthavimsamsa, Trimsamsa, Khavedamsa, Akshavedamsa and Shashtiamsa."
> — BPHS 6.2–4

The Rasi (D1) is the chart itself. The other fifteen are subdivisions of the 30° sign, each producing a second sign assignment.

## How the divisional lagnas are read

A varga lagna is read as the orientation of *that* life theme. A strong, well-placed Navamsa lagna with its lord well-placed reads as a strong marriage and dharmic life; a strong Dasamsa lagna reads as strong career foundations. The classical reading is composite — the rasi lagna establishes the overall chart, and each varga lagna confirms or qualifies the reading for its theme.

A planet that retains the same sign or trinal sign across rasi and navamsa is termed *vargottama* and is treated as especially strong in that placement; the same logic extends to the other vargas. Parasara treats vargottama strength explicitly under Sthaana Bala in Chapter 27 (the *Saptavargaja Bala*), showing the connection between divisional placement and the shadbala calculation.
