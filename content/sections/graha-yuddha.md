## What this section measures

*Graha Yuddha* — planetary war — is the classical doctrine that when two of the five "starry" planets (Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn) come within close conjunction, one is read as the victor and the other as the loser. *BPHS* Chapter 27 treats it briefly within the shadbala calculation:

> "Should there be a war between the starry planets (i.e. between 2 planets from Mars to Saturn, in a given horoscope), the difference between the Shadbalas of the two should be added to the victor's shadbala and deducted from the Shadbala of the vanquished."
> — BPHS 27.20

Saravali expands on the doctrine across multiple slokas, providing the criteria used to determine the victor. The five starry planets only — the Sun and Moon are not subject to *Graha Yuddha* in the strict sense, and Rahu and Ketu (being shadow points rather than physical bodies) are not assigned to wars.

## How a war is identified

The standard convention identifies a planetary war when two of the five eligible planets are within approximately 1° of each other in longitude. Some lineages use a 1°00' orb; others use 0°59' or even tighter. DashaflowView reports any pair within the standard orb.

Each war detected is shown with:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Planet 1 / Planet 2 | The two planets engaged in the war |
| Separation degrees | The exact angular separation |
| Winner | The planet read as the victor |
| Loser | The planet read as weakened |

## How the victor is determined

Classical authority gives several criteria, often applied in order. The most commonly cited:

| Criterion | Rule |
|---|---|
| Latitude / brightness | The planet with the higher absolute latitude or the brighter visible appearance wins |
| Northward direction | The planet to the north of its opponent wins |
| Larger size | The visually larger planet wins |
| Higher dignity | The planet in stronger dignity wins |

The rule applied in DashaflowView's calculation follows the standard convention based on visible brightness and northern displacement. Different lineages weight these criteria differently; the calculation is one defensible reading rather than the unique correct answer.

## How the section is read

A planet on the losing side of a *Graha Yuddha* is treated as weakened in delivering its significations during the periods it rules. *BPHS* 27.20 makes this quantitative: the difference in shadbala between the two planets is added to the victor and subtracted from the loser. A loser that was already weak in shadbala becomes notably weaker; a loser otherwise strong remains functional but reduced.

The reading is composite. A *Graha Yuddha* loser that nonetheless ends with high shadbala, good dignity, and favourable yoga participation can still produce its results. Conversely, a winner whose underlying placement is weak does not gain absolute strength — the war calculation modifies the shadbala but does not overwrite the chart's other layers.

The section renders only when a war is present. A chart with no planets within the orb shows no entries here, and the section is omitted from the chart UX.
