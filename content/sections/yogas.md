## What this section measures

A *yoga* is a named combination of planetary placements that classical authority recognises as producing a specific characteristic result. *BPHS* devotes Chapters 34 through 44 to yogas — over ten consecutive chapters cataloguing them by category, signalling the centrality of the system in Parasara's framework.

DashaflowView lists the yogas present in your chart, distinguishing major yogas (highlighted) from minor yogas. The list is not exhaustive of every classical yoga in the literature — it covers the named combinations Parasara, Saravali, and the broader tradition treat as the most consequential.

## The categories of yoga

Classical authority groups yogas into recognisable categories, each with its own chapter or section in *BPHS*:

| Category | Source chapter(s) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Yoga Karakas | BPHS Ch. 34 | Planets that produce the effects of multiple yogas through their placements |
| Nabhasa Yogas | BPHS Ch. 35 | Pattern-based yogas (planets in specific geometric configurations) |
| Lunar Yogas | BPHS Ch. 37 | Yogas formed by the Moon's relationship to other planets — *Sunapha*, *Anapha*, *Durdhura*, *Kemadruma* |
| Solar Yogas | BPHS Ch. 38 | Yogas formed by the Sun's relationship to other planets |
| Pancha Mahapurusha | Saravali, BPHS | The five "great person" yogas formed by Mars (*Ruchaka*), Mercury (*Bhadra*), Jupiter (*Hamsa*), Venus (*Malavya*), and Saturn (*Shasha*) when each is in its own sign or exaltation in a kendra |
| Raja Yogas | BPHS Ch. 39, 40 | Combinations producing royal or high-authority effects — typically the conjunction or aspect of kendra and trikona lords |
| Dhana Yogas | BPHS Ch. 41 | Combinations producing wealth — typically the connection of the 2nd, 5th, 9th, and 11th lords |
| Sannyasa Yogas | (covered in Yoga literature) | Combinations indicating renunciation |
| Other named yogas | scattered | *Gajakesari* (Moon and Jupiter in mutual kendra), *Adhi Yoga*, *Lakshmi Yoga*, *Vipareeta Raja Yoga* |

## What the section shows

Each yoga in the list shows three things:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Name | The classical name of the yoga |
| Formed by | The planet or planets that participate in the yoga |
| Description | The classical reading of the yoga's effect |

Major yogas (those Parasara and the tradition treat as most consequential) are visually highlighted in the chart UX. The current rendering treats the following as major: *Malavya*, *Shasha*, *Bhadra*, *Hamsa*, *Ruchaka* (the five Pancha Mahapurusha), *Gajakesari*, *Raj Yoga*, *Lakshmi Yoga*, and *Adhi Yoga*. Other yogas are rendered in a subdued style.

## How the section is read

A yoga is read as a structural feature of the chart that supplements the planet-by-planet and house-by-house readings. A chart with several major yogas is structurally rich; a chart with a few well-formed yogas in good condition can produce the named effects strongly even if individual planets are middling.

Yoga readings are conditional on three things: whether the participating planets are well-placed (dignity, shadbala), whether the relevant lords aspect or conjoin appropriately, and whether competing yogas (particularly *Vipareeta Raja Yoga* combinations involving the dustana lords 6, 8, 12) qualify the reading. A yoga formed but afflicted by a malefic aspect or by a debilitated participating planet delivers a partial result.

The tradition emphasises that yoga readings must be interpreted in context — the same Gajakesari yoga in two charts can deliver very different results depending on what else is happening across the chart's other layers. The yogas listed here are inputs to a composite reading, not standalone verdicts.
