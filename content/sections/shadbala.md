## What this section measures

Shadbala is Parasara's scheme for putting a number on the strength of each planet. *Brihat Parasara Hora Sastra* Chapter 27 sets out six independent measurements, applied to the seven planets from Sun to Saturn — the nodes Rahu and Ketu are not assigned shadbala. The six measurements are added together and compared against a fixed threshold per planet. A planet that meets its threshold is read as strong enough to deliver its significations during the periods it rules.

## The six strengths

### Sthaana Bala — positional strength

The strength a planet acquires from where it sits — its sign and divisional placements. Parasara opens the chapter with this and builds it up from five sub-strengths: *Uchcha Bala* (closeness to exaltation), *Saptavargaja Bala* (placement across the seven divisional charts from *rasi* to *trimsamsa*), *Ojhayugmarasiamsa Bala* (odd or even placement in sign and *navamsa*), *Kendradi Bala* (angularity — strength from being in a kendra, panaphara, or apoklima house), and *Drekkana Bala* (decanate). [BPHS 27.1–6]

### Dig Bala — directional strength

The strength a planet acquires from being in the house most favourable to it. Each of the seven planets has one such house. Sun and Mars peak on the tenth-house cusp (the meridian); Jupiter and Mercury on the Lagna; Venus and Moon on the fourth-house cusp (the nadir); Saturn on the seventh-house cusp (the descendant). The strength scales linearly from full at the preferred cusp to zero at the opposite cusp. [BPHS 27.7–7½]

### Kala Bala — temporal strength

The strength a planet acquires from when it operates. *Kala Bala* is itself a composite of seven sub-strengths derived from the day of the week, the lunar fortnight, the part of the day or night, the year, the month, the day, and the planetary hour: *Natonnata*, *Paksha*, *Tribhaga*, *Abda*, *Masa*, *Dina*, and *Hora* balas. [BPHS 27.8–17]

### Cheshta Bala — motional strength

The strength a planet acquires from its motion through the zodiac. For the Sun, *Cheshta Bala* is computed from his *Ayana Bala*; for the Moon, from her *Paksha Bala*. For the five star planets (Mars through Saturn), the calculation uses the angular distance between mean and true longitude — which in practice tracks retrograde behaviour and the speed of motion. [BPHS 27.18, 27.24–25]

### Naisargika Bala — natural strength

The constant baseline strength of each planet, derived from its natural luminosity. Parasara assigns a fixed value to each — in *rupas*: Sun 1.000, Moon 0.857, Venus 0.714, Jupiter 0.571, Mercury 0.429, Mars 0.286, Saturn 0.143. The brightest planet gets the highest value; the darkest, the lowest. [BPHS 27.14]

### Drik Bala — aspectual strength

The strength a planet acquires or loses from being aspected by other planets. Benefic aspects add a quarter of the *Drishti Pinda*; malefic aspects subtract a quarter. The aspects of Mercury and Jupiter are added in full. [BPHS 27.19]

## How the score is read

The six strengths are summed in *virupas*, where one *rupa* equals sixty *virupas*. Parasara fixes the threshold each planet must reach to be counted strong:

> "390, 360, 300, 420, 390, 330 and 300 Virupas are the Shadbala Pindas needed for the Sun etc. (upto Saturn) to be considered strong. If the strength exceeds, the planet is deemed to be very strong."
> — BPHS 27.32–33

Expressed in *rupas*: Sun 6.5, Moon 6.0, Mars 5.0, Mercury 7.0, Jupiter 6.5, Venus 5.5, Saturn 5.0. Mercury carries the highest bar; Mars and Saturn the lowest.

The two rightmost columns of the table — *Ishta Phala* and *Kashta Phala* — are derived measures Parasara treats in the next chapter. *Ishta* is the benefic potential a planet can deliver during its periods; *Kashta* is the malefic potential. [BPHS Ch. 28]
