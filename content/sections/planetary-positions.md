## What this section measures

This section reports where each planet sits at the moment of birth and the classical qualifiers that govern how each planet's effects are read. Nine planets are tracked: Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu, and Ketu (the lunar nodes). The first seven are the *graha*s in the strict sense; Rahu and Ketu are the shadow points where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic, treated by classical authority as planets for interpretive purposes.

*BPHS* Chapter 3 sets out the names, characters, deities, sex, complexions, dignities, friendships, exaltation and debilitation points, and other definitional attributes of each planet. The interpretive layer rendered here draws on those definitions.

## What each column measures

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Planet | One of the nine: Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu, Ketu |
| Sign | The zodiac sign the planet occupies (sidereal, Lahiri ayanamsha) |
| Degree | The exact position within the sign (0°–30°) |
| House | The bhava of the rasi chart (1 through 12) |
| Nakshatra | The lunar mansion (one of 27) the planet occupies |
| Pada | The quarter (1–4) of the nakshatra |
| Dignity | The planet's relationship to the sign it occupies (see below) |
| ℞ | Retrograde — the planet is moving backwards in the zodiac |
| Combust | The planet is too close to the Sun to be visible from Earth |

## The dignity system

Classical authority assigns each planet a graded relationship to each sign. *BPHS* Chapter 3 (Natural Relationships) and Chapter 4 (Zodiacal Signs) set out the system. The standard hierarchy from strongest to weakest is:

| Dignity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exalted | The sign of greatest strength for the planet (e.g., Sun in Aries, Moon in Taurus, Saturn in Libra) |
| Mooltrikona | The sign of "root strength" — second only to exaltation, in a specific degree range |
| Own | The planet rules the sign it occupies |
| Friend | The sign is owned by a planetary friend |
| Neutral | The sign is owned by a neutral planet |
| Enemy | The sign is owned by a planetary enemy |
| Debilitated | The sign of greatest weakness (the sign 180° from exaltation) |

DashaflowView colour-codes the dignity column accordingly: green for exalted/own/mooltrikona, teal for friend, grey for neutral, orange for enemy, red for debilitated.

## Retrograde and combust modifiers

Retrograde motion (℞) is treated in classical authority as an intensifier of the planet's significations rather than as a malefic modifier. The motion is computed against true longitude versus mean longitude — the planet is moving "backwards" relative to the Earth's perspective. *BPHS* Chapter 27 treats retrograde behaviour formally as part of *Cheshta Bala* (motional strength).

Combust (●) means the planet is within a specific orb of the Sun and effectively invisible from Earth. The classical reading treats combust planets as having their effects obscured or "burnt" by the Sun's intensity. The orb varies by planet — Mercury's orb is approximately 12°, the larger planets approximately 7°.

The Sun cannot be combust (it is the source of combustion) and the Moon and the nodes are not subject to standard combustion in the same sense. Among the planets that are subject to combustion, the reading must qualify dignity and shadbala — a planet exalted but combust loses much of its delivery.

## How the section is read

The Planetary Positions table is the foundation row most other sections build on. The dignity column is read first — it sets the baseline strength of each planet. Retrograde and combust modifiers qualify the dignity reading. House placement determines the area of life the planet's effects fall in. Nakshatra and pada feed the Vimshottari Dasha system and the finer-grained sub-readings.

The reading of each planet is composite with the rest of the chart. A planet's effects are determined by its dignity (this section), its strength (Shadbala), its state (Avastha), the yogas it participates in (Yogas), the dasha period it is currently active in (Vimshottari Dasha), and its bindu charge in the sign it occupies (Ashtakavarga). The Planetary Positions table is the entry point — every other section refines or qualifies what is read here.
