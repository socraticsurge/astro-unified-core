## What this section measures

The panchang is the five-limb description of any moment in time — the same framework the Vedic calendar uses to fix muhurtas (auspicious times for events) and to mark festivals. Applied to the moment of birth, the panchang tells you the calendrical context the chart was born into. The five limbs are *vara* (day), *tithi* (lunar phase), *nakshatra* (lunar mansion), *yoga* (combined Sun-Moon configuration), and *karana* (half-tithi).

For a birth chart, the panchang serves two purposes. It anchors the chart in the Vedic calendar — useful for determining which festivals or vows the birth coincides with. And it surfaces the most consequential timing inputs the rest of the chart will use: the Moon's nakshatra in particular determines the Vimshottari Dasha sequence, which is why it is visually highlighted in this section.

## The five limbs

### Tithi (lunar phase)

The tithi is the angular separation between the Sun and the Moon measured in increments of 12°, producing 30 tithis per lunar month — 15 in the bright fortnight (*shukla paksha*, waxing) and 15 in the dark fortnight (*krishna paksha*, waning). Each tithi has a name, a presiding deity, and classical associations. The tithi at birth is read in the tradition as a temperamental indicator: certain tithis (Purnima, Amavasya, Ashtami, Chaturdashi) are treated as more challenging than others.

### Vara (weekday)

The vara is simply the day of the week. Each weekday is ruled by one of the seven classical planets:

| Day | Lord |
|---|---|
| Sunday | Sun |
| Monday | Moon |
| Tuesday | Mars |
| Wednesday | Mercury |
| Thursday | Jupiter |
| Friday | Venus |
| Saturday | Saturn |

The vara lord contributes to the *Kala Bala* (temporal strength) calculation in shadbala — being born on a planet's day strengthens that planet.

### Nakshatra (lunar mansion)

The nakshatra is the segment of the zodiac (one of 27, each 13°20' wide) that the Moon occupies at the moment of birth. The nakshatra at birth determines the Vimshottari Dasha lord (see the Vimshottari Dasha section). Each nakshatra has a presiding deity, a planetary lord, and a set of classical associations covering the temperament, profession, and life themes of those born under it.

The pada — the quarter of the nakshatra — further refines the reading. Each nakshatra has four padas of 3°20' each, mapped to the navamsa.

### Yoga (Sun-Moon combined position)

The *yoga* in the panchang sense (distinct from the *yoga* in the chart-combination sense) is the sum of the Sun's and Moon's longitudes, divided into 27 segments of 13°20' each. Each yoga has a name and a classical reading, treated in the tradition as a quality of the moment that colours all activity undertaken under it.

### Karana (half-tithi)

The karana is half a tithi (6° of Sun-Moon separation). Eleven karanas cycle through the lunar month — seven movable and four fixed. Like the yoga and tithi, the karana of birth is read as a calendrical quality of the moment.

## How the section is read

The Moon's nakshatra is the most consequential single value in the panchang. It determines the Vimshottari Dasha sequence — the timing structure the rest of the life unfolds on. DashaflowView visually highlights the nakshatra card for this reason.

The other four limbs are read together as the calendrical "weather" the chart was born into — qualifying the chart's reading rather than driving it. A chart born on Sunday with the Sun strongly placed gets a small additional reinforcement of the Sun's significations; a chart born on the day of an inauspicious tithi inherits some of that tithi's classical association.

For working interpretation, the panchang section is read first — its values feed the dasha and house-strength readings — and then revisited when timing-specific questions arise (when to undertake a particular activity, which transits to watch, what muhurtas align with the natal panchang).
